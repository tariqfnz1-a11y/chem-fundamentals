Branches of Chemistry

Chemistry is one subject wearing seven different hats, depending on what question you're asking.

Notes

  • Organic — chemistry of carbon compounds (fuels, plastics, medicines)
  • Inorganic — everything else: metals, minerals, salts
  • Physical — energy, rates, and how/why reactions happen
  • Biochemistry — chemistry happening inside living things
  • Industrial — chemistry at factory scale
  • Nuclear — reactions inside the atomic nucleus
  • Environmental — chemistry of air, water and soil
✎ Think of it like…

…a hospital. The cardiologist, radiologist and dietician are all "doctors," but each looks at the same patient through a different lens. Organic, physical, nuclear chemists all look at the same matter — just through different questions.

☕ Everyday life

Cooking dinner uses organic chemistry (the food) and physical chemistry (heat changes). An X-ray uses nuclear chemistry. A water filter uses environmental chemistry.

Atoms · Ions

Atoms, Molecules & Ions

Three related building blocks — and the difference is all about counting particles and charge.

Notes

  • Atom — smallest unit of an element that still behaves like that element
  • Molecule — two or more atoms bonded together (same element, like O₂, or different, like H₂O)
  • Ion — an atom (or group of atoms) that has gained or lost electrons, giving it a charge
  • Gain electrons → negative ion (anion). Lose electrons → positive ion (cation)
✎ Think of it like…

Lego bricks. One brick = an atom. Snap two identical bricks together = a molecule like O₂. Snap different bricks together = a molecule like H₂O. Now imagine a brick with a magnet stuck on (extra electron) or one magnet missing (lost electron) — that's an ion.

☕ Everyday life

Table salt is made of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. The oxygen you breathe is O₂ molecules. A gold ring is just a huge collection of gold atoms.

Atomic Mass

Atomic Mass Unit & Relative Atomic Mass

Why chlorine's atomic mass is 35.5 and not a whole number.

Notes

  • amu (u) — a tiny unit of mass, defined as 1/12 the mass of one carbon-12 atom
  • Relative atomic mass — average mass of an element's atoms, weighted by how common each isotope is
  • Relative molecular mass — sum of relative atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule
✎ Think of it like…

The "average shoe size" of a classroom. If 75% of students wear size 8 and 25% wear size 9, the class average isn't a clean number — it's 8.25. Chlorine's 35.5 comes the same way: mostly Cl-35 atoms, with some heavier Cl-37 mixed in.

☕ Everyday life

Any "average" you compute daily — average marks, average rainfall — works the same math as relative atomic mass.

Avogadro's Number, Mole, Molar Mass & Molar Volume

A chemist's way of counting the uncountable.

Notes

  • Mole — a counting unit for 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number)
  • Molar mass — mass of one mole of a substance, in grams (numerically = atomic/molecular mass)
  • Molar volume — one mole of any gas occupies 22.4 L at STP
✎ Think of it like…

A "dozen" for eggs, or a "ream" for paper (500 sheets). Atoms are far too small and numerous to count one by one — so chemists count them in one giant batch: the mole. Molar volume is like saying "a dozen tennis balls and a dozen golf balls both = 12 balls," except with gases, a mole of any gas fills the same 22.4 L box at STP because the particles themselves take up almost no space compared to the gaps between them.

☕ Everyday life

A helium balloon, the CO₂ in a fizzy drink, the O₂ in a hospital cylinder — all measured in moles behind the scenes.

Empirical & Molecular Formulas, Percentage Composition

Reducing a compound to its simplest recipe — and then scaling it back up.

Notes

  • % composition — mass % of each element in a compound
  • Empirical formula — simplest whole-number ratio of atoms
  • Molecular formula — the real number of atoms (a whole-number multiple of the empirical formula)
✎ Think of it like…

A recipe written as "2 cups flour : 1 cup sugar" instead of "8 cups flour : 4 cups sugar" — same ratio, simplest form. Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) is just the empirical formula CH₂O multiplied by 6 — the "full recipe" instead of the "reduced" one.

☕ Everyday life

A fertilizer bag listing "20-10-10" (N-P-K) is percentage composition. A food label's "% Daily Value" works the same way.

Basic Stoichiometric Calculations

Using mole ratios from a balanced equation to predict how much product forms.

Notes

  • A balanced equation gives mole ratios between reactants and products
  • The limiting reactant runs out first and caps how much product can form
  • Whatever's left of the other reactant is the excess
✎ Think of it like…

Making sandwiches: 2 bread slices + 1 cheese slice = 1 sandwich. With 10 bread slices but only 3 cheese slices, cheese runs out first — you can only make 3 sandwiches, and 4 bread slices sit unused. Cheese is the "limiting reactant."

☕ Everyday life

Assembling furniture with a fixed number of screws, or baking a cake — you can only make as many batches as your scarcest ingredient allows.
